ScrollQ — train on the best first

September 2026 Vesuvius Progress Prize submission by Sven (solo) + Muse (AI assistant). Repo: Svyable/scrollq (MIT) · Live leaderboard

1. What did you do?

Built ScrollQ, a data-quality triage system for the Vesuvius scroll volumes. It decodes real voxels from every scroll volume — via the vendored volcomp decoder, no approximations — and scores each volume 0–100 on signal presence, texture energy, dynamic range, and an acquisition-artifact (dead-slice) scan. All 64 scroll volumes on dl.ash2txt.org scored, published as a ranked leaderboard with every component exposed.

2. How does this increase the probability of reading complete scrolls?

Segmentation is the bottleneck: expert hours and GPU time are scarce, and every volume costs both. ScrollQ directs that scarce effort where the data is healthiest. The headline finding: all 70 published ink-detection labels sit on PHercParis4 — quality rank 13 of 39 — while the healthiest volumes (PHerc0813 at 77.4, PHerc0139 at 75.9 with 114 segments but zero ink labels) have no labels at all. The leaderboard flags 16 top-quartile volumes as 🎯 label next: the highest-leverage labeling targets in the open data. Same scanner, same settings produced both the best and worst volumes — data health varies more than hardware does.

4. Honest scope

ScrollQ is a triage signal, not a readability claim. It does not detect ink and does not predict which scroll reads first. The weights are a judgment call, published with every score — audit them, don't worship the ranking. During development a naive dead-slice rule false-positived on mask geometry; it was tightened and the clean bill above is what survived verification.

Companion entry: zarr-pyramid-audit ("don't train on lies" — corruption detection for the open data). ScrollQ is "train on the best first." Together they are one data-quality suite: scrollq-health runs the integrity audit and the quality score on any volume and issues a single verdict — TRAIN / CAUTION / DO NOT TRAIN.

1. What did you do?

Built ScrollQ, a data-quality triage system for the Vesuvius scroll volumes. It decodes real voxels from every scroll volume — via the vendored volcomp decoder, no approximations — and scores each volume 0–100 on signal presence, texture energy, dynamic range, and an acquisition-artifact (dead-slice) scan. All 64 scroll volumes on dl.ash2txt.org scored, published as a ranked leaderboard with every component exposed. The project is evolving into ScrolIQ, an open diagnostic layer for the Challenge's 2026 open problems: scroliq-scan-map preserves spatial provenance instead of collapsing observations into one score, scroliq-passport turns one volume's evidence into a machine-readable record, and a Grand Prize probe protocol scores targets against auditable evidence. Alignment: docs/open-problems-alignment.md.

2. How does this increase the probability of reading complete scrolls?

Segmentation is the bottleneck: expert hours and GPU time are scarce, and every volume costs both. ScrollQ directs that scarce effort where the data is healthiest. The headline finding: all 70 published ink-detection labels sit on PHercParis4 — quality rank 2 of 39 scrolls — while the healthiest volumes (PHerc0172 at 78.3, PHerc0139 at 76.3 with 114 segments but zero ink labels) have no labels at all. The leaderboard flags 16 top-quartile volumes as 🎯 label next: the highest-leverage labeling targets in the open data. Same scanner, same settings produced both the best and worst volumes — data health varies more than hardware does.

This does not clear our 0.85 gate. Per-chunk provenance (stability-n24-dense-prov.json) proves the "disjoint" resample is not truly disjoint: only 2/64 volumes had zero chunk overlap between runs; mean Jaccard = 0.63 (63% of chunks re-read on average). The sampling loop scans all candidates until N chunks decode, so sparse volumes re-read the same present shards. The ρ = 0.79 is therefore inflated by re-reading — true disjoint-sample stability is lower, and we do not claim otherwise. Per-chunk score distributions show a mean within-volume std of 18.5 points (range 4–33): the average scroll's chunks span nearly a full quality tier. We publish the ranking because a representative measurement with stated uncertainty beats no measurement — but read each score with its spread, not as a point estimate. 4. Honest scope

ScrollQ is a triage signal, not a readability claim. It does not detect ink and does not predict which scroll reads first. The weights are a judgment call, published with every score — audit them, don't worship the ranking. During development a naive dead-slice rule false-positived on mask geometry; it was tightened, and the clean bill above is what survived verification.

5. Known-success control

PHerc1667 — the Challenge's first completely virtually unwrapped and read scroll — now serves as a scroll-level control for the spatial scan metrics. Its p10–p90 intervals overlap the targets' substantially (nonzero fraction Jaccard ≈ 1.0 vs PHerc0813), which is a guardrail, not a score: any metric value inside the control's range cannot be called a failure on another scroll.
